  <p class="home-hero__tagline">Hi, I'm Gautham<span class="home-hero__accent">.</span> Welcome to my personal page.</p>

  <p class="home-hero__intro">I'm a postdoctoral researcher at Armagh Observatory &amp; Planetarium, where I study massive stars &mdash; their mass loss, wind-driving physics, and how these outflows shape stellar structure and evolution across every phase of a massive star's life.</p>

  <p class="home-hero__intro">I study the evolution of massive and very massive stars using the <strong>MESA</strong> stellar evolution code, with a wind framework implementation dedicated to VMS evolution in both the <a href="https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022MNRAS.514.3736S/abstract">local</a> and <a href="https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023MNRAS.524.1529S/abstract">early</a> Universe. I'm equally interested in the winds that drive this mass loss: I use the <strong>PoWR</strong> atmosphere code for hydrodynamical modelling of stellar winds, most recently deriving <a href="https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2026arXiv260728012S/abstract">new mass-loss predictions</a> for very massive stars, such as <a href="https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv251213782S/abstract">the wind kink</a> where winds transition from optically thin to optically thick.</p>

  <p class="home-hero__intro">I completed my PhD in Astrophysics at Armagh Observatory &amp; Planetarium (2019&ndash;2023), supervised by Prof. Jorick S. Vink, with a thesis on theoretical studies of massive stars near the Eddington limit. Before that, an MSc in Physics at the University of Leeds, and a BEng in Mechanical Engineering at NIT Karnataka, India.</p>
